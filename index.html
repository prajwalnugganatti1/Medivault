🔬 The Enological Chemistry Behind Wine Quality

Wine evaluation combines subjective sensory perception with objective chemical compounds. Based on research by Cortez et al., 2009 at the University of Minho, the dataset models the relationship between 11 physicochemical measurements and expert sensory ratings:

1. Volatile Acidity (Acetic Acid - Spoilage Indicator)

Acetic acid creates vinegar odor and acrid taste. High volatile acidity (>0.65 g/dm³ in reds, >0.45 g/dm³ in whites) is the single most direct chemical defect signaling bacterial spoilage (Acetobacter).

2. Alcohol & Density (Body and Structure)

Higher natural alcohol percentages (11.5% - 13.5% vol) strongly correlate with premium vintages, providing body, flavor extraction, and perceived sweet warmth that balances tannins.

3. Sulphates & Sulfur Dioxide (Preservation Stability)

Potassium sulphate (K₂SO₄) acts as an antioxidant and antimicrobial stabilizer. Adequate free SO₂ prevents premature oxidation (browning and sherry-like off-flavors), while excessive total SO₂ yields unpleasant burnt-match aromas.

4. Fixed Acidity, Citric Acid & pH (Crispness & Equilibrium)

Fixed acids (principally tartaric and malic) maintain proper pH (ideally 3.0 to 3.6). Citric acid imparts palate freshness. When pH climbs above 3.7, wines feel flat, flabby, and vulnerable to spoilage.
